# iPhone-Duo-MagSafe-Wallet
Instructions and files for the a 3d printed wallet that uses a trimmed back panel from an existing Apple MagSafe Wallet.
Below are instructions on how to do this. This will fit about 2-3 cards. 

iPhone Duo MagSafe Wallet — Insert Trimming & Assembly
Overview
The finished wallet is 66.6 × 87.6 × 6.6 mm (2.62 × 3.45 × 0.26 in), holds 3 cards upright, and fits inside the folded iPhone Duo's 84.1 × 117.8 mm (3.31 × 4.64 in) back. It reuses the magnet back panel of an old Apple MagSafe wallet, trimmed to 63 × 84 mm (2.48 × 3.31 in), so the magnets and its built-in spring tab keep working.

Parts

  • Front tray and back plate: wallet_63x84_two_part.stl (both parts on one plate)
  
  • Insert: back panel of an Apple MagSafe wallet, trimmed to 63 × 84 × 2 mm (2.48 × 3.31 × 0.08 in)

Tools and materials

  • Sharp hobby knife with a fresh blade, and a metal ruler
  
  • Calipers (or a good mm ruler)
  
  • CA glue, gel type (for plastic to plastic) or regular PLA glue works

  • A flat, heavy object or two small clamps
  
  • A few standard cards for test fitting
  
Trimming the Apple wallet into an insert
The insert is the wallet's magnet back panel cut free along both long seams, then shortened to 84 mm (3.31 in) at the spring-tab end. The far end keeps Apple's original rounded corners. These steps are reconstructed from the photos of the finished insert.
  1. Free the back panel. Cut along both long edges, just inside the seam where the front card pocket joins the back panel. Use several light passes with a fresh blade.     Keep the full panel width; it should come out about 63 mm (2.48 in) wide.
  2. Find the parts you must not cut. On the fabric-lined side you'll see the spring tab (a trapezoid flap) near one end, the oblong hole in the middle, and a raised U-shape around the hole. Run a steel paperclip over the panel to feel where the magnets are. Keep every cut clear of the magnets and the tab.
  3. Mark the length. Measure 84 mm (3.31 in) from the original rounded end, toward the spring-tab end. The mark should land about 3 mm (0.12 in) past the tab's narrow free end. If it lands closer than about 2 mm (0.08 in), cut a little longer and leave the tab's own cut lines intact.
  4. Cut the end square. Hold a metal ruler on the mark and cut straight across in several light passes. Don't try to cut through in one stroke; the layers will shift.
  5. Round the new corners. Trim the two new corners to about a 3.5 mm (0.14 in) radius. The recess in the back plate has rounded corners, and square insert corners won't sit flat.
  6. Clean the edges. Trim loose threads and ragged leather along the long seams. A thin wipe of CA along the cut edges stops fraying, but keep it off the spring tab.
  7. Check it. The insert should measure 63 × 84 × 2 mm (2.48 × 3.31 × 0.08 in), lie flat, and the spring tab should flex freely. Its wide end is the hinge; its narrow end lifts.
  8. 
In the rest of this guide, the tab end of the insert is the bottom and the rounded end is the top.

Printing the two parts
Print both parts flat from wallet_63x84_two_part.stl with no supports: nothing in either part overhangs.

Settings
Textured PEI (gives the outer face its finish)
Layer height
0.2 mm (0.008 in)
Supports
Off
Walls
4

Material
PLA or PETG ( I used PLA)
  • Front tray (outer face and card-pocket walls) prints face down. Its bed edge is cut at 45° so it doesn't sag.
  • Back plate (floor and insert recess) prints floor down, with the recess facing up. It has the spring-tab opening, the push-out hole, four pin holes and six small crush ribs on the recess walls.
After printing, clear any strings from the pin holes and check the four pins on the tray aren't blobbed.
Dry fit before gluing

Assemble everything without glue first; once glued, the tray and plate can't come apart.
  1. Press the back plate onto the front tray, floor side down, so the four pins drop into the holes. The outer edges should line up all the way round.
  2. Press the insert into the recess, leather (phone) side up. Put the tab end at the bottom, the end away from the card slot. The tab's wide hinge then faces the card slot and its narrow free end points down.
  3. Check the insert's oblong hole lines up with the push-out hole in the floor.
  4. Slide in 1 card, then 3. One card should be held by the spring; three should still slide in and out. Cards ride over the hinge first, so they shouldn't catch.
  5. Push the cards out through the oblong hole with your thumb.
If the insert is too tight, scrape the crush ribs down with a hobby knife. If the spring doesn't hold a single card, bend the tab up slightly by hand; it needs to lift about 2.7 mm (0.11 in).

Gluing the front tray to the back plate
Use CA gel on the tray's wall tops only, and keep every drop out of the card pocket.
  1. Take the insert back out.
  2. Lay the front tray face down. Apply small dots of CA gel along the tops of the two side walls and the narrow bottom strip, keeping about 2 mm (0.08 in) away from the pocket edge.
  3. Lower the back plate on, floor side down, guiding the pins into their holes.
  4. Press flat on the table with a heavy book, or clamp, for 1–2 minutes.
  5. Right away, slide a card in and out once. If it drags, glue has crept into the pocket; clear it with the card before it cures.
  6. Wipe any squeeze-out on the outside edges and let it cure for at least 15 minutes.

Gluing in the insert
Glue the insert to the floor around its edges only, so the spring tab and both holes stay free.
  1. Choose the glue. E6000 or contact cement bonds leather to plastic and stays flexible, but needs about 24 hours. CA gel sets in a minute but can harden and darken the leather. I used the PLA gel and weighed it down overnight, holding fine.
  2. Put small dots around the floor's outer edge. Keep at least 3 mm (0.12 in) away from the tab opening and the push-out hole.
  3. Press the insert in, leather side up and tab end at the bottom. The crush ribs hold it in line while the glue sets.
  4. Press flat under a weight. For E6000, press for a few minutes, then leave it 24 hours. For CA, press for 1 minute.
  5. Before it cures, slide a card in and out a few times; if the spring tab feels stiff, glue has reached its hinge, so free it before the glue cures.
